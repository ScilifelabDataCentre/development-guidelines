# FAQs (Frequently Asked Questions)

## What is the difference between a proposal and a roadmap?

A proposal is written before substantial work has begun on a project or service. It is very short and
does not describe the plan of work for a fixed period - it describes, in brief, roughly what will exist  
and why.

A roadmap is a high-level plan of work for a fixed time period. It is often longer than a proposal. It is
not a one-time document: roadmaps will continue to be written as long as the service exists.

## What is the difference between an ADR and these project management documents?

An Architectural Decision Record (ADR) is about 'how' we are going to implement a specific part of an application. It is
technical and will be both written and read by technical stakeholders. Non-technical stakeholders are not interested
in this level of detail about _how_ we will implement something. The software developers have the leading role in an
ADR.

In contrast, a proposal or roadmap document rarely talks about the 'how' (and, if it does, it will be at a high-level) -
they are both about the 'what' (_What will this service do for end-users? What problem will it solve?_). Non-technical
stakeholders are often interested in this information. The Product/Service Owner has a leading role in these documents.
